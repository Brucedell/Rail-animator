10.2 Loco and tender: one vehicle or two?

Two vehicles, coupled ✓ (recommended)

  • The tender swings on its own drawbar, as in reality.
  • It reuses the ordinary bogie-car template.
  • Tender-first running, or uncoupling for servicing, comes for free (Part 8).
  • The pair can be published as a consist preset asset, so it drops in as a unit.

One vehicle with six pivots

  • Simpler outliner.
  • But the tender's body then has to be solved from pivots inside a loco-vehicle. That means a second body, which the data model doesn't have.
  • Uncoupling becomes impossible.

10.5 Pony and trailing trucks

A pony truck is an axle on a radius arm pivoting on a pin fixed to the frame. The rig reproduces exactly that:

TRUCK_F   (child of nothing; driven entirely by constraints)
  Copy Location  → LEAD_TRUCK Empty           axle stays on the rail
  Damped Track   → FRAME, head/tail offset to the pin (+2.60)   arm points at the pin
                   (or: a PIN_F bone, child of FRAME at +2.60, as the Damped Track target)
  Copy Rotation  → LEAD_TRUCK Empty, roll only           takes the rail's cant
WHL_F_L / WHL_F_R   (children of TRUCK_F) : rotation from Auto driver, Ø 0.97 m
Why not just Copy Transforms from the Empty?

The Empty's heading is the rail tangent at the axle, which is correct for the wheelset but not for the truck frame. The truck frame must point at its pin, otherwise the radius arm visibly detaches from the loco frame on curves. Use the Empty for position and roll, and the pin for yaw.

10.6 Wheels and quartering

  • Two wheel groups: drivers (Ø 1.73 m, r = 0.865 m) and trucks (Ø 0.97 m, r = 0.485 m). Same distance, different rotation rates, both from the Auto driver d / r.
  • All four drivers share one angle (they are coupled by rods). Drive DRIVERS_ANGLE once, and let each driver bone copy it, so a slip animation spins them together.
  • Quartering: the right-hand crank pins sit 90° ahead of the left-hand ones (a common convention, check your prototype). Build it into the crank pin placement (the right side bone rotated +90°), not into the drivers, so wheel rotation stays a single value.

10.7 Rods and valve gear

Coupling rods

The coupling rod joins the crank pins of D1 and D2. Because both pins turn together, the rod translates without rotating: bone ROD_CPL_L = child of FRAME, Copy Location from CRANK_D1_L. It stays parallel to the frame automatically.

Main (connecting) rod: slider-crank

Walschaerts valve gear (optional depth)

PartRigging approach
Return crank / eccentric rodReturn crank on the crank pin (fixed offset angle ~90°). Eccentric rod: Damped Track from the return-crank end to the expansion link's lower end.
Expansion linkRocks about a fixed trunnion on the frame. Its angle comes from the eccentric rod end (Damped Track, or a Transform constraint).
Radius rod + die blockThe die position in the link is the reverser: a custom property reverser (−1 … +1) that the animator keys for forward/back gear and cut-off. The radius rod runs from the die to the combination lever.
Combination lever / union linkHangs from the radius rod; its bottom follows the crosshead via the union link. Two-bone IK with a pole works well here.
Make reversing visible

Tie reverser's default to the sign of speed with a driver: sign(Δd), smoothed. The valve gear then flips into back gear when the train reverses, and the animator can override it. Few rigs bother with this, and viewers notice.

10.8 The tender

  • An ordinary bogie car: two TRACKED pivots at ±2.40 m; the body on the chord (Part 6 §6.4).
  • Each bogie bone takes Copy Transforms from its pivot Empty. Its two wheelsets are children at ±0.80 m, rotation Auto, Ø 0.97 m.
  • Drawbar: a bone on the loco's rear coupler with Damped Track to a target bone on the tender's front coupler. Its length change on curves is tiny, and a Stretch To set to volume “none” hides it if needed.
  • Fall-plate and coal/water details are children of the tender BODY. Add some sway (FX) that is slightly out of phase with the loco for realism.

10.10 Walkthrough: unrigged → in the framework

  1. Prepare both models

    Metres, +Y forward (loco chimney toward +Y), apply transforms, rail top at Z = 0. Keep the loco and tender as separate collections.

  2. Split the loco into parts

    Frame, boiler, cab, each wheel (8), crank pins, coupling rods (2), main rods (2), crossheads (2), piston rods (2), valve gear parts, and truck frames (2). Set each origin at its rotation centre (wheels at the axle, rods at their pin ends, crosshead at its slide centre).

  3. Measure

    Run Measure Vehicle, or read origins by hand: axle Ys (+3.95, +1.05, −1.05, −3.95), wheel diameters (1.73 / 0.97), and the pin positions of the truck arms.

  4. Make Vehicle → “Steam loco”

    Enter the Whyte notation 2-4-2. The template creates the root at the centre of the driving wheelbase, two AXLE_POINT pivots at the drivers, two RADIAL pivots at the truck axles with pin offsets, and the rig from 10.9 (without valve gear). Check the gizmos in the viewport.

  5. Rods

    Add the crosshead drivers (Fig. 10.3), the coupling rod Copy Locations and the quartering on the crank bones. Then optionally Add Walschaerts gear and wire reverser.

  6. Bind meshes and rig

    Name-based auto parenting, then Bind Rig. Hide the pivot Empties.

  7. Tender

    Make Vehicle → “Bogie car” with bogies at ±2.40 m, wheelsets at ±0.80 m within each bogie, Ø 0.97 m. Bind.

  8. Couple and publish

    Create a consist [Loco, Tender gap 0.30]. Publish the loco and tender as vehicle assets, and the pair as a consist preset asset (Track It / Consists / 242T + Tender).

  9. Test drive

    Run it on the test track: minimum radius (check the truck-to-cylinder clearance), the banked curve (average of cant across the drivers), the turnout (the trucks swing through independently), a reverse run (valve gear flips, rods run backwards), and tender-first running (flip the loco's facing and move the tender to the front of the consist).

  10. Animate a shot

    Key the turnout at the station throat, then the consist's distance: depart, accelerate, cruise and brake with the Speed Profile tool. Add a wheel-slip burst on starting (slip_angle keys on DRIVERS_ANGLE with grip at 1), and tune FX amounts.

10.11 Pitfalls and checks

SymptomCause and fix
Pony truck wheels drift off the rail on curvesThe truck was given Copy Transforms from the Empty and is parented to FRAME. Remove the parent: trucks are driven by constraints only.
The body cuts the inside of curvesThe body is following the root instead of the driver chord. Use FRAME from 10.4.
Rods jitter or flip at the dead centresIK used for the main rod. Use the exact slider-crank driver.
Wheels skid visuallyWrong diameter group, or the model wasn't in metres. Re-measure; check with the tyre-marker test.
The tender lags or leads by a few cm after a switchLegacy odometer drift (today's consist prototype). Fixed by the occupied-path evaluator (Part 5).
Rods turn backwards when reversingThey shouldn't: they follow d, which decreases. If they do, a driver uses abs(), or facing was applied twice.

10.12 What this study adds to the backlog

  • Pivot roles (RADIAL with pin_offset, AXLE_POINT) in TrackIt_VehiclePivotItem.
  • A FRAME/BODY chord solver generated by Bind Rig.
  • Multiple wheel groups per vehicle, and a shared driver angle.
